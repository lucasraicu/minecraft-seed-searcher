Codex’s revised Master Plan is remarkably thorough, and it **directly incorporates the majority of the technical conclusions** from our audit—especially the 97% terrain bottleneck, the 8-block Y-noise cell reuse, the 25–30 GiB memory guards, and the strict validation gates.

However, there are **6 specific architectural and implementation details from our audit that are NOT mentioned, left unresolved, or treated differently in Codex's plan**:

---

### 1. Integrating Lower-32 Sweeping as the Front-Door Funnel (Phase Ordering)
*   **What Codex's Plan Does:** Codex is very skeptical of lower-32 filtering because it fears missing a great full-64 seed. Consequently, it relegates the Lower-32 C sweep all the way to **Phase D (Tasks D1–D3)** as an optional "selection experiment" and defaults to searching full-64 seeds from the start.
*   **What was in our Audit:** In high-performance seed searching, sweeping the 4.29 billion lower-32 structure space is the **front-door coarse filter** (Tier 0). Discarding lower-32 seeds that don't have a Fortress, Bastion, Village, and Ancient City in the target bounding box culls 99.9% of the search space in seconds, preventing millions of wasted 64-bit biome and terrain calculations.

---

### 2. Moving 4×4 Quart Land Reuse Directly into C (`bridge.c`)
*   **What Codex's Plan Does:** Codex acknowledges that 4×4 quart reuse saved 28.1% of time in historical Python scripts ([SIMD_TEST_RESULTS.md](file:///Users/lucasraicu/Downloads/minecraft-seed-searcher/SIMD_TEST_RESULTS.md)), and tasks **B2** targets the *8-block vertical Y-noise reuse*.
*   **What is Not in Codex's Plan:** Codex does not specify moving the **horizontal 4×4 quart reuse directly into the native C `ss_land` function**. Currently, quart reuse is only handled in Python wrappers (`scripts/audit_system.py` and `cache.py`). Pushing the 4×4 cell deduplication down into C eliminates thousands of CFFI pointer conversions per seed.

---

### 3. Concrete Worker/Memory Topology for the Intel Xeon Host
*   **What Codex's Plan Does:** Codex notes: *"Compare process × native-thread combinations... Treat these as trial points, not recommended defaults; leave CPU headroom when BDS runs"* ([Task C1](file:///Users/lucasraicu/Downloads/minecraft-seed-searcher/MASTER_IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN.md#L133)). It leaves the topology entirely open-ended.
*   **What was in our Audit:** The audit specifically addresses your host machine (**Intel Xeon W-2191B 18 cores / 36 threads with a 32 GB RAM ceiling**). Running Codex's current default of **30 single-threaded Python workers** while simultaneously running a **6 GiB Docker BDS container** pushes total RSS right against the 30 GiB OOM threshold. The audit explicitly defines the optimal topology: **12–16 Python workers with 2 OpenMP threads each**, leaving 4–6 dedicated CPU threads and 8+ GiB of RAM completely free for Docker BDS.

---

### 4. A Concrete Priority Queue for Fast Discovery
*   **What Codex's Plan Does:** Codex states in **B4**: *"Keep approximate prefilters in shadow mode... Use them only for scheduling or explicitly labeled shadow predictions"*.
*   **What is Not in Codex's Plan:** Codex does not define *how* that scheduling works. In our audit, we mapped out a concrete **Min/Max Priority Queue** based on `approx_height` and structure proximity. Instead of scanning seeds sequentially in index order ($0, 1, 2, \dots$), seeds are pushed into an in-memory priority heap so that the most promising candidate seeds are evaluated and surfaced within the first 5 minutes of a sweep.

---

### 5. Root-Cause Explanation of the 71/256 BDS Terrain Errors
*   **What Codex's Plan Does:** Codex records the symptom: *"Selected actual 256-column check: 71 land/water disagreements, height MAE 3.805 blocks, maximum error 39"* ([Table in Section 3](file:///Users/lucasraicu/Downloads/minecraft-seed-searcher/MASTER_IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN.md#L53)). It mandates that we calibrate against BDS.
*   **What is Not in Codex's Plan:** Codex doesn't explain the underlying game-engine causes:
    1. **3D Aquifers and local water levels:** Bedrock generates independent water tables inside caves and mountains that raw density does not know about.
    2. **Cave Carvers (cheese/spaghetti caves):** They carve out solid ground that the predictor marks as land.
    3. **Surface Decoration:** Grass blocks, sand, waterlogging, and foliage layers shift the topmost surface block by 1–5 blocks compared to raw mathematical noise.

---

### 6. Structure Bounding Box Overlap / Intersection Logic
*   **What Codex's Plan Does:** Codex updated the policy in Section 1 to: *"Final Nether requires **Fortress AND Bastion**. Do not restore the old optional-Bastion rule."* Both are required to generate somewhere inside the `1024 × 1024` Nether square.
*   **What is Not in Codex's Plan:** As we discussed earlier, Codex does **not** include any logic to test if the Fortress and Bastion **physically touch or intersect** each other. It only checks that both exist within the square. If you want them physically connected/overlapping, that intersection check is completely absent from Codex's plan.

---

### Recommendation
Codex's plan is an exceptional baseline for safety and verification. We should adopt Codex's **Phase A (Verification & Baseline Freezing)** and **Phase B (Noise Reuse Optimization)**, but we should incorporate these 6 enhancements—specifically moving the 4×4 quart reuse into C, establishing the 14-worker / 2-OpenMP thread topology, and scheduling work via a priority queue.
