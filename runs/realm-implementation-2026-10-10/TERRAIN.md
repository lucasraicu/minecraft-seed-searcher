# Realm terrain implementation evidence — 10 October 2026

Implemented `realm/raster.py` and `realm/terrain.py` without changing legacy raster semantics or launching BDS. V1 and V2 consume the same saved qualified baseline pool independently. Diagnostic inclusion of pending baseline pairs is explicit (`terrain.include_pending`); normal terrain consumes PREDICTED/VERIFIED pairs. Terrain never changes baseline eligibility.

The half-open Raster interface carries height, known coverage, optional surface biome, explicit water and natural-ground channels, and provider metadata. Cache identity includes signed decimal seed, provider identity, dimension, settings, bounds, spacing and nomination/height stage. Unsupported or missing coverage stays unknown. A native smoke test sampled seed `42` at half-open `[-64,-64,65,65)` with spacing 64 through both inexpensive 2D nomination and density-height stages; each returned a 3×3 PREDICTED raster and retained its sea-level proxy limitation. Actual water is used when supplied; elevated lakes do not require ocean biome IDs. Certification requires resolution 1, actual block water, complete coverage and verified natural-ground provenance. Current BDS structure provenance remains PARTIAL.

V1 labels conservative eight-neighbor land components and four-neighbor water components, rejects connected peninsulas/thin observed bridges, uses area-equivalent effective diameter `2*sqrt(island_area/pi)` for qualification and ideal scores, and records the sampled cell-footprint convex-hull maximum span descriptively, counts a common water component across 32 physical rays and measures fractional cell areas. The local formation footprint includes the island and relevant water belt. A bounded lake belt extends through observed opposite shores using the maximum closed-ray width, so a 240-block lake enclosure is not incorrectly capped at the ideal 100 blocks. The distance belt excludes unrelated longer lake arms/streams; component bounds and the footprint policy are saved separately. Open water without observed outer shores supplies an explicitly labeled minimum local belt and unknown complete-formation fit, rather than claiming an entire ocean fits. Geometry qualification is independent of footprint completeness. Observed overflow remains outside even when the full footprint is unresolved; expansion sizes then remain lower bounds. Default minimums remain diameter 250–750, area 12000, 24 enclosing rays and 48-block median width. Default ideal targets and 30/55/15 geometry weights follow the plan. No mountain or ocean-biome gate is added.

V2 measures ridge relief relative to median interior elevation, radial support width/area, face/diagonal connected support and adjacent ray continuity, with the largest uninterrupted angular gap saved. Defaults remain 20 rays, 35 relative relief and distance 300–850, with ideal 26 rays, 55 relief and 350–750 distance. Isolated spikes do not supply enclosure. Interior roughness is descriptive; V2 has no flatness, buildability, floor-area minimum or floor score. Remaining enclosure/access weights normalize to 87.5/12.5. Ridge labels extend beyond the nomination distance annulus: the maximum qualifying ray distance does not clip the physical formation. Supporting connected ridge cells are measured within a local halo of eight configured support widths beyond the maximum distance. Support continuing beyond that halo, touching the raster edge, or neighboring missing cells makes the full footprint incomplete. Geometry may still qualify, but fit remains unknown unless measured overflow already proves outside. A 500–1100-block ridge annulus around a 900-halfwidth Realm is now retained outside, with its actual observed support extent, rather than falsely reported within an 850-clipped footprint.

Center proposals combine component centroids or local minima with an unsnapped 64-block grid across the entire 2688-square Realm. No Stronghold distance cap applies. Analysis halos extend beyond mandatory bounds. Cheap 128-block 2D nomination runs before heavy density sampling; selected centers refine on the configured 64/32/16/8/4 schedule, with a 16-block local center neighborhood. Each variant has its own refinement budget. Low geometry scores at spacing 32 or finer defer fine work and preserve the baseline; the default scheduling score is 40. Zero refinement budget performs no generation and leaves tasks pending. Fine rasters are restricted to selected local windows. Both nomination halos and local radii derive from merged configured V1/V2 thresholds, including the supporting-ridge halo, rather than fixed default constants. The shared sampling lattice remains stable when one variant finishes before another.

Every center result is committed independently. Aggregate task completion permits a completed-task resume to skip provider construction and all sampling. Completed pairs do not charge the invocation work limit; repeated `--limit 1` calls advance through unfinished pairs instead of stopping forever on the first complete pair. Partially committed tasks replay deterministically and upsert without duplicate centers. Scores use configured geometry/biome/baseline weights (default 80/10/10) and fixed baseline cap 60. Biome fit uses fixed family denominators, with Savanna neutral. Predicted, verified and partial actual score fields are separate. Complete-formation border fit is separate from geometry, with overflow, minimum centered size and add-on-compatible size; incomplete footprints retain unknown fit unless already observed outside, and expansion sizes remain lower bounds. Source hashes for terrain/raster/biomes and terrain/provider/game/settings configuration are pinned separately from baseline identity before any completed-task resume. A mismatch errors explicitly. Pure score weights have a separate persisted identity; explicit weight-only rescore acknowledges its update without regenerating terrain.

### Focused scoring correction (2026-10-10 continuation)

`terrain.apply_score_policy` now applies the same configurable score weights and uncertainty rules to prediction, CLI actual evaluation, and saved-metric rescore. Numerical biome/baseline inputs alone cannot certify their evidence. Actual verification obtains biome fit only from supplied raster biome IDs; absent IDs remain UNKNOWN with zero contribution, and the predicted biome fit is not copied. Older unknown-provenance actual biome inputs are retained as `unverified_biome_fit_input` and excluded from the actual score. Baseline evidence retains the candidate's qualification. Only complete formation coverage plus VERIFIED evidence for each positively weighted component produces a verified score.

The prediction remains the outer saved view with its independent `predicted_score`; generated evidence remains nested in `actual_evaluation`, with independent `actual_partial_score` or `verified_score`. Rescore updates both views using recorded inputs and the saved policy, without generation. Results ranking selects the nested actual geometry/score only when `score_verified_complete`; otherwise the prediction ranking is retained and actual scores stay separately visible. CSV exports expose all three score channels, while JSONL preserves the nested evidence and policy. CLI verification and terrain both honor saved rescore weights.

### Terrain sampling-provider resume identity (backlog #4)

Persistent terrain checkpoints now pin the selected native extension's bytes, `backend.py`, `seeds.py`, and the backend manifest, in addition to the existing terrain/raster/biome source and computational configuration identity. Extension discovery uses `importlib.util.find_spec` and filesystem hashing, without loading/rebuilding the extension or constructing a generator. The check runs before completed tasks are skipped, even when the invocation has no unfinished work. A changed or missing native build fails explicitly and leaves existing records/identity untouched.

Custom provider factories must declare a static, nonempty JSON identity before persistent sampling or resume. Supply `run_terrain(..., provider_factory=factory, provider_identity={'provider': 'name', 'build': 'version', 'extractor': 'version'})`, or set `factory.terrain_identity` to that declaration; a static `factory.cache_identity` is also accepted. Identity suppliers cannot be callable or contain process-local objects/nonfinite numbers. The declaration must cover every custom provider build, model, extraction setting, and behavior not already represented by the Realm configuration. Provider instances are not constructed to discover identity. Distinct factory functions with the same declared build can resume the same completed task; callable object addresses are never used. Stateless test stores without persistent state APIs retain their existing factory API, without a persistent resume provenance guarantee.

Historical terrain results or started/completed tasks without a pinned terrain identity cannot be silently adopted under a newly discovered provider. They fail with an instruction to use a separate terrain checkpoint; their saved evidence remains available for inspection/export. Old algorithm identity schemas also fail explicitly, preserving their history. Weight-only rescore identity remains separate.

Changes: `realm/terrain.py`, new `tests/test_realm_terrain_identity.py`, and explicit synthetic provider declarations in the existing RealStore terrain fixtures. Deterministic tests cover native binary/wrapper changes, missing extensions, stable custom identities across distinct factories, factory identity attributes, settings changes, undeclared/nonstatic custom identities, rejection before completed-task skipping, zero provider calls for unchanged completed tasks, and preservation of historical records. No native generation, BDS run, build, or benchmark occurred.

### V2 enclosing arc continuity controls

The previous adjacent-bearing rule accepted substantial disconnected hills: eight radius180 hills at radius600 (24 supported rays), six radius240 hills at650 (22), four radius350 hills at650 (20), and eight broad radial ellipses240x180 at600 (24). Each met the agreed ray/relief/distance tests despite being a collection of independent hills. New deterministic controls reproduced all four false accepts before changing eligibility.

V2 now contributes a ray only when its same-component contiguous circular bearing run forms an elongated supporting arc. Configurable engineering defaults are `ridge_min_arc_rays=4` and `ridge_min_arc_aspect=2.0`. Aspect is estimated tangential arc length (run angular bins × median component radius) divided by robust radial thickness (90th−10th radial percentiles inside the nomination annulus, plus one sample width). Circular runs cross bearing zero correctly. The substantial hill controls have run lengths3–5 and aspect1.07–1.62; four separated enclosing arcs have7-ray runs/aspect7.44. Components lacking substantial tangential support cannot combine into qualifying enclosure. Measurements and rejected component IDs are retained per run for inspection.

This does not require a connected closed mountain ring: two separate arcs, four separate arcs, a90°open pass, and jagged-floor separated arcs remain positives. Existing20/32 ridge rays, relative relief35, distance300–850, max gap12, and87.5/12.5 geometry weights remain unchanged. No floor flatness/buildability gate or floor score was introduced. The ratio/run defaults are sampled engineering policy, not exact calibrated natural-mountain classification. Actual-world calibration remains outstanding. Independent predicted near matches and baseline records remain preserved.

`config/realm.yaml` includes both new defaults. Validation accepts older V2 configs lacking these two keys without mutating persisted configuration; the detector merges defaults at evaluation. Validation rejects unknown geometry keys, and requires the arc-ray setting to be an integer within the ray count. Source algorithm identity still rejects stale existing terrain tasks rather than adopting them.

### Durable terrain work, retries, and fair bounded resume

Persistent `RealmStore` runs now use one explicit frozen baseline pool revision and independent V1/V2 center/stage work queues. Nomination commits every proposal's coarse metrics and successor work, not only the current invocation's best eight. The configured budget counts distinct center chains per variant per invocation. Every completed resolution saves full evaluated metrics, the selected center, raw exact-lattice raster channels, and successor work atomically. Repeated invocations advance untouched/deferred centers or finer stages without repeating committed stage sampling. The legacy stateless-store path remains only a fixture/API compatibility fallback.

Prediction rasters are compressed NPZ/base64 observations with exact bounds, resolution, lattice, height, known mask, optional biome/water/natural-ground channels, and metadata. Stable, fully known, explicitly labeled prediction observations can be decoded by another variant or resumed invocation without constructing a generator. Actual snapshots have pool-revision/variant/stage scope and are never automatically reused as a fluid-stability cache. Source-unknown, failed, unsupported, missing, or unstable samples cannot enter the immutable shared prediction cache or complete an empty nomination queue. They retain attempt-scoped raw evidence, closest/current PENDING geometry, and a retryable unit through atomic storage deferral. Unit/attempt-specific IDs avoid failed V1/V2 journal collisions, and deferral checks the current lease owner and attempt. Natural-ground uncertainty remains separate from valid stable actual raw coverage; `raster_variant_fit` supplies truthful actual biome provenance.

Incomplete finest formations retain local halo successors. The default maximum of two automatic halo expansions is a resource policy, not task completion. Later work remains DEFERRED with needed bounds; `continue_deferred_halos=True` authorizes one further stage per center/invocation. No mandatory Realm bounds or baseline records are expanded or removed. Aggregate tasks become complete only when their durable queue is completely evaluated; blocked manual work is reported separately. Progress and final output distinguish queued pending/deferred work from pool members whose variant work has not yet been initialized.

A persisted cursor per pool revision and requested variant set rotates bounded `--limit 1` runs across membership IDs, using indexed `start_after`/`stop_at` reads without decoding skipped records. Cursor advancement is saved when a stage is claimed, so even a provider exception before its first observation cannot permanently starve later anchors. Abandoned leases recover under an exclusive scheduler lock; uncommitted work remains pending/retryable. Individual V1 and V2 invocation cursors remain independent. Explicit API keywords are `pool_revision`, `new_pool_revision`, and `continue_deferred_halos`. Terrain identity pins the new work schema and observation encoding in addition to source/provider/config identity; old task identities are not adopted.

Final focused verification: 122 passed in 3.35s across scheduler, work storage, work CLI, terrain, provider identity, V2 continuity, scoring, raster reuse, CLI reports, and rescore tests. Eighteen scheduler cases cover failed/unsupported/missing/source-unknown/unstable nomination recovery, partial height metrics, interruption recovery, independent budgets, full-channel exact-lattice roundtrip, prediction sharing, actual fresh snapshots, failed-attempt collision avoidance, halo cap/manual continuation, rotating cursor/reopen/independent variants, provider exceptions, and uninitialized pool reporting. Existing completed-task fixtures now explicitly propose one resolved center and use bounded lake data; pending-many-center behavior has its own new controls. No BDS execution, native generation, rebuild, or benchmark was performed in this batch.

Remaining verification/resource scope: production native timing and cache parity have not been measured after these changes; synthetic tests do not establish exact Bedrock formation classification or calibrated recall. Actual natural-ground/whole-formation certification and broader population calibration remain separate backend-dependent gates.
